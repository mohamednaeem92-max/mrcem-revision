# Anatomy Batch 31 Research Notes

## Decision standard

The original `Anatomy-All.pdf` source pages control the literal stem, option sequence, and marked answer. The references below only corroborate medical correctness. A source-confirmed record is restored only where the exact formulation has adequate external support. A wording-sensitive, contradictory, or non-unique record remains `needs_review`, `askable:false`, with no answer or learning aid.

| Record | Source-page marked option | External corroboration or conflict | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| `p0952-q0066` | 4, **Para-aortic nodes** | NCBI states that para-aortic, also called lumbar-aortic, nodes drain the testes.[1] | **Restore.** The key and wording are directly corroborated. |
| `p0955-q0067` | 0, **L1** | The lumbar-plexus reference describes the ilioinguinal nerve as deriving from L1.[2] However, a separate NCBI StatPearls reference describes origin from anterior rami of T12 and L1,[3] which conflicts with the source stem's single-level formulation. | **Restrict.** Standard teaching supports L1, but the exact source wording does not account for documented root variation and the offered options omit T12–L1. |
| `p0960-q0068` | 2, **lies posterior to the first part of the duodenum** | A peer-reviewed biliary-anatomy review states that the common bile duct courses posterior to the first portion of the duodenum.[4] A second review describes the retroduodenal portion as passing behind the superior part of the duodenum.[5] | **Restore.** The source key and exact relation are corroborated. |
| `p0963-q0069` | 3, **Tail of pancreas** | NCBI places the tail anterior to the left kidney and at the splenic hilum.[6] A peer-reviewed surgical-anatomy review places the pancreatic tail in the splenorenal ligament.[7] | **Restore.** The tail's splenorenal-ligament relation supports it as the non-retroperitoneal option in this set. |
| `p0966-q0070` | 4, **Spleen** | The source page visibly marks this option. External material confirms the splenic flexure is inferior to the spleen and documents transverse-mesocolon continuity,[8] but no authoritative source reviewed provides a direct, complete posterior-relation list sufficient to test all five options exactly. | **Restrict.** The exact all-options relational formulation is insufficiently corroborated, despite the displayed source key. |
| `p0971-q0071` | 0, **Preprostatic urethra** | A cadaveric dissection article states that the small preprostatic urethral segment is surrounded by the vesical, or internal urethral, sphincter.[9] NCBI separately locates the preprostatic urethra between the bladder and prostate.[10] | **Restore.** The key and structure relation are directly corroborated. |
| `p0975-q0072` | 0, **Hepatorenal recess** | NCBI identifies the hepatorenal recess in the RUQ FAST view and describes the RUQ as most likely to detect free fluid.[11] However, the ACEP reference also states that, in a supine woman, the pelvic pouch of Douglas is a dependent peritoneal site and that free fluid may be seen there first.[12] | **Restrict.** The source stem asks for a single most-likely space in a woman without specifying the FAST view or amount of fluid; authoritative references support more than one plausible dependent recess. |
| `p0979-q0073` | 1, **Uterine tubes** | NCBI states that about 97% of ectopic pregnancies occur in the fallopian tube and that the ampulla is the most frequent tubal location.[13] ACOG also identifies the fallopian tube as the commonest ectopic site.[14] | **Restore.** “Uterine tubes” correctly captures the anatomic site; the ampulla is a subsite. |
| `p0982-q0074` | 1, **The shoulder tip** | NCBI states that the phrenic nerve arises from C3–C5, provides sensation to the central tendon of the diaphragm, and refers diaphragmatic pain to the shoulder tip.[15] | **Restore.** The central diaphragmatic sensory pathway and referred location are adequately corroborated. |
| `p0985-q0075` | 2, **S2–S4** | NCBI identifies bladder parasympathetic fibers as arising from S2–S4 via the pelvic splanchnic nerves and states that they contract detrusor muscle for voiding.[16] A peer-reviewed review independently confirms the S2–S4 pelvic-splanchnic pathway.[17] | **Restore.** The source key and exact spinal-segment range are directly corroborated. |

**Seven** records are eligible for source-clean restoration as unapproved, mock-ineligible `ocr_draft` records: `p0952-q0066`, `p0960-q0068`, `p0963-q0069`, `p0971-q0071`, `p0979-q0073`, `p0982-q0074`, and `p0985-q0075`.

**Three** records remain excluded `needs_review` records: `p0955-q0067` due to root-variation conflict, `p0966-q0070` due to an insufficiently corroborated exact relation set, and `p0975-q0072` due to a non-unique dependent-recess formulation. Their direct source review will remain documented, but they must not receive a correct option or learning aid.
