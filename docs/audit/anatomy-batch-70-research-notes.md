# Anatomy Batch 70 Research Notes

Batch 70 covers ten Central Nervous System records from `Anatomy-All.pdf`, source pages 1915–1924. All ten source pages were inspected directly. External corroboration is restricted to authoritative NCBI Bookshelf references.

The cerebral-cortex and sensory-cortex references support question 61: two-point discrimination is a cortical sensory function associated with the parietal lobe, while frontal-lobe lesions can produce contralateral weakness, personality change, expressive dysphasia, and conjugate eye deviation. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, The Anatomy of the Cerebral Cortex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK575742/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Somatosensory Cortex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK555915/).

The ventricular-system reference supports question 62: the lateral ventricles connect to the third ventricle through the interventricular foramina of Monro, not the cerebral aqueduct. It also supports choroid-plexus production and ventricular relationships. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Ventricular System](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532932/).

The vertebral-artery and posterior-circulation references support question 63: each vertebral artery arises from the corresponding subclavian artery. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Anatomy, Head and Neck, Vertebral Arteries](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441972/).

The internal-capsule reference supports question 64: the internal capsule is a compact collection of projection fibres connecting the cerebral cortex with subcortical structures, brainstem, and spinal cord. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Internal Capsule](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542181/).

The Broca-area reference supports question 65: Broca area is in the dominant inferior frontal gyrus and supports expressive language. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Broca Area](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK526096/).

The brainstem-nuclei reference supports question 66: facial, abducens, trigeminal, and vestibulocochlear nuclei are associated with the pons, whereas the trochlear nucleus lies in the midbrain. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Brainstem](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544297/).

The cerebellar-dysfunction reference supports question 67 and the displayed DANISH signs: dysmetria/dysdiadochokinesia, ataxia, nystagmus, intention tremor, scanning/slurred speech, and hypotonia. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Cerebellar Dysfunction](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562317/).

The visual-pathway reference supports questions 68 and 70: a right parietal optic-radiation lesion causes left homonymous inferior quadrantanopia, while parietal optic-radiation fibres serve the contralateral lower visual field. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Visual Field Deficits](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10912/).

The retinal vascular reference supports question 69: the ophthalmic artery arises from the internal carotid artery; its central retinal artery is an end artery, and embolic occlusion can cause acute monocular blindness with a cherry-red spot. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Central Retinal Artery Occlusion](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470354/).
