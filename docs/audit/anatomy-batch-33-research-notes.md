# Anatomy Batch 33 External Evidence Notes

| Record | Candidate source key | External corroboration or limitation | Provisional decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| `p1033-q0086` | Proximal duodenum | NCBI lists the first duodenal part as intraperitoneal and adrenal glands, ascending colon, descending colon, and oesophagus as retroperitoneal. A peer-reviewed radiology review specifies that the proximal 2 cm of the first part is the sole intraperitoneal duodenal segment. The question’s wording is broad but the displayed key is consistent with the authoritative classification. [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK534788/) [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7315055/) | Restore candidate. |
| `p1036-q0087` | L1 - L4 | NCBI identifies the lumbar plexus as formed from the anterior rami of L1–L4, with a possible T12 contribution. A peer-reviewed anatomical study states that the plexus originates from ventral rami L1–L4. [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK564296/) [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2813498/) | Restore candidate. |
| `p1040-q0088` | Membranous urethra | NCBI describes the membranous urethra as passing through the external urethral sphincter. A peer-reviewed sphincter review states that the male external sphincter is located at the membranous urethra and is formed by circular skeletal muscle fibers inferior to the caudal prostate. [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542238/) [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3469827/) | Restore candidate. |
| `p1044-q0089` | Puborectalis muscle | NCBI states that puborectalis forms a sling around the anorectal junction and maintains the anorectal angle, an anatomic barrier to stool discharge. A peer-reviewed review describes this muscle as a principal continence mechanism and reports faecal incontinence when it is damaged or malfunctions. [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459128/) [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8801252/) | Restore candidate. |
| `p1048-q0090` | Left kidney | NCBI describes the kidneys as retroperitoneal at T12–L3 and notes that the overlying twelfth rib commonly crosses the upper pole. It also directs assessment of kidney injury with lower-rib trauma. A CT study confirms that the left twelfth rib is close to the kidney but warns that spleen or colon can intervene in some individuals. The displayed key is the best supported option, but the learning aid must not imply that this surface relation is invariant. [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482385/) [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK541020/) [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10406120/) | Restore candidate with variation caveat. |
| `p1050-q0092` | Common bile duct | NCBI defines choledocholithiasis as stones within the common bile duct and states that common-bile-duct obstruction causes pain, jaundice, sepsis, and may cause cholangitis. The source triad of fever, right-upper-quadrant pain, and jaundice is therefore concordant with the displayed key. [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441961/) [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539698/) | Restore candidate. |
| `p1053-q0093` | Transversalis fascia | NCBI identifies the deep/internal inguinal ring as an opening formed by the transversalis fascia. A second NCBI surgical anatomy reference calls it a lateral hiatus within that fascia. [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470204/) [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459309/) | Restore candidate. |
| `p1057-q0094` | L1 | The original source displays L1, but the current NCBI ureter anatomy reference places the ureteropelvic junction, where renal pelvis transitions to ureter, roughly at L2. NCBI kidney anatomy also puts the renal hilum at L2. The source’s exact L1 key is therefore not adequately corroborated. [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532980/) [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482385/) | Restrict: exact level conflicts with current authoritative anatomy. |
| `p1060-q0095` | Umbilical region | NCBI states that midgut pain refers to the umbilicus. A peer-reviewed peritoneal-innervation review specifically classifies jejunum and ileum as midgut structures with periumbilical referral. [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK553104/) [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6328075/) | Restore candidate. |
| `p1063-q0096` | Anterior rami of L1 - L3 | The original source displays L1–L3. A current NCBI iliopsoas reference supports short collateral lumbar-plexus branches L1–L3, and another NCBI posterior-wall reference also lists L1–L3. However, the dedicated NCBI psoas-major reference instead reports anterior rami L1–L4 plus small femoral branches. The exact isolated L1–L3 wording is therefore not sufficiently stable for restoration. [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK531508/) [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557605/) [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK535418/) | Restrict: conflicting authoritative root-level descriptions. |
