## Evidence disposition

The NCBI cerebral-hemisphere and cerebral-cortex chapters state that the parietal lobe lies posterior to the central sulcus and superior to the lateral sulcus, supporting question 42. They describe the frontal lobe’s medial and lateral cortical territories and the anterior and middle cerebral artery supply, supporting question 43. The same references locate the dominant-language Broca area in the inferior frontal gyrus and describe the frontal motor cortex, supporting questions 45 and 47. They also associate parietal-lobe injury with sensory and spatial deficits, supporting question 48. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Cerebral Hemisphere](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549789/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, The Anatomy of the Cerebral Cortex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK575742/).

The NCBI dorsal-column and spinal-cord references support question 44: fine touch, vibration, and proprioception are carried by the posterior-column–medial-lemniscus pathway. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Posterior Column](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507888/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Spinal Cord](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559056/).

The peer-reviewed visual-pathway review and NCBI bitemporal-hemianopsia reference support questions 46 and 49. The occipital lobe is posterior/inferior to the parieto-occipital sulcus, while optic-chiasm lesions interrupt crossing nasal retinal fibres and cause bitemporal hemianopia. Sources: [Patel et al., Imaging of the Primary Visual Pathway based on Visual Deficits](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8053434/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Bitemporal Hemianopsia](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545213/).

The NCBI precentral-gyrus/cerebral-hemisphere anatomy references support question 47’s left frontal localization for right-hand motor impairment with slow, effortful expressive speech. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Precentral Gyrus](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544218/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Cerebral Hemisphere](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549789/).

The NCBI cervical spinal-root and spinal-cord anatomy references support question 50’s C7 answer. C7 is associated with elbow extension and the middle-finger sensory distribution in the clinical root/myotome pattern. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Spinal Cord](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559056/).

The NCBI ventricular-system reference supports question 51: CSF is reabsorbed through arachnoid granulations into the dural venous sinuses. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Ventricular System](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532932/).
