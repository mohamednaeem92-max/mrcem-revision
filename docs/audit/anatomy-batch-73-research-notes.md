# Anatomy Batch 73 Research Notes

Batch 73 covers ten Anatomy records from `Anatomy-All.pdf`, source pages 1989, 1995, 2000, 2001, 2005, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2015, and 2017. All ten source records were inspected directly.

The facial-nerve and internal-auditory-meatus references support question 15: CN VII leaves the brainstem at the cerebellopontine angle and enters the internal acoustic meatus before the facial canal. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Facial Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK526119/).

The trigeminal reference supports questions 16, 17, and 20. The mandibular division carries motor fibres to muscles of mastication and general sensation from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue; it exits via foramen ovale. Orbicularis oculi is supplied by CN VII, not V3. The upper lip is supplied by V2 rather than V3. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Trigeminal Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507716/).

The oculomotor and trochlear references support questions 18 and 10 in the source sequence. CN III traverses the lateral cavernous sinus, enters the orbit via the superior orbital fissure, supplies four extraocular muscles and parasympathetic fibres to sphincter pupillae, and does not supply dilator pupillae. CN IV supplies superior oblique. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, Oculomotor Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537126/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Trochlear Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537173/).

The vagus reference supports question 19 and the glossopharyngeal reference supports question 21. A unilateral vagus lesion causes ipsilateral palatal weakness with the uvula deviating away from the affected side. CN X carries visceral afferents from the aortic arch and is the efferent gag-reflex limb; CN IX is the afferent limb and supplies the parotid secretomotor pathway. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, Vagus Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537304/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Glossopharyngeal Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539877/).

The maxillary-division and lingual-nerve references support questions 22–24. V2 supplies general sensation to the upper lip, upper teeth, hard palate, and nasopharynx, not the external ear. Facial palsy can cause hyperacusis through stapedius weakness. Lingual nerve injury causes loss of general sensation over the anterior two-thirds of the tongue while taste and salivation remain intact when chorda tympani fibres are preserved. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, Maxillary Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544268/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Lingual Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK546626/).
