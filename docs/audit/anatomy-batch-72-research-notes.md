# Anatomy Batch 72 Research Notes

Batch 72 covers ten Anatomy records from `Anatomy-All.pdf`, source pages 1949, 1953, 1958, 1962, 1964, 1968, 1971, 1975, 1981, and 1985. All ten source records were inspected directly. Question 14 on page 1985 is image-dependent and will remain marked `needs_image` and non-askable unless the source image is preserved by the existing media workflow.

The glossopharyngeal and vagus references support questions 5 and 13: CN IX provides the afferent limb of the gag reflex, while CN X provides the efferent limb; CN IX exits through the jugular foramen, innervates stylopharyngeus, and carries secretomotor fibres to the parotid through the otic ganglion. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, Glossopharyngeal Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539877/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Vagus Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537304/).

The cerebellopontine-angle and internal-auditory-meatus references support questions 6 and 8. Vestibulocochlear and facial nerves traverse the internal auditory meatus and are commonly involved by cerebellopontine-angle lesions. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Acoustic Neuroma](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470469/).

The abducens, oculomotor, and trochlear references support questions 7, 9, and 10. CN VI originates in the pons, enters the orbit through the superior orbital fissure, supplies lateral rectus, and palsy causes a convergent squint with horizontal diplopia. CN III palsy causes a down-and-out eye with ptosis. CN IV supplies superior oblique. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, Abducens Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537129/), [NCBI Bookshelf, Oculomotor Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537126/), and [NCBI Bookshelf, Trochlear Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537173/).

The spinal-accessory and facial-nerve references support questions 11 and 12. CN XI supplies sternocleidomastoid and trapezius; the facial nerve divides into its terminal motor branches within the parotid gland, although it does not provide the gland’s secretomotor supply. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, Accessory Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537304/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Facial Nerve](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK526119/).

The source image for question 14 visibly demonstrates a convergent squint with the affected eye turned inward, consistent with abducens palsy and loss of lateral-rectus function. It will be preserved as a visual dependency rather than replaced with text-only reconstruction. All restored records remain unapproved OCR drafts and mock-excluded.
