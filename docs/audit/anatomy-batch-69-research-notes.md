# Anatomy Batch 69 Research Notes

Batch 69 covers ten Central Nervous System records from `Anatomy-All.pdf`, source pages 1903–1912. All ten source pages were inspected directly. External corroboration is limited to authoritative NCBI Bookshelf references.

The ventricular-system reference supports question 52: CSF is primarily produced by the choroid plexus within the ventricles. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Ventricular System](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532932/).

The cerebral-cortex and language references support questions 53, 54, and 55. Broca area lies in the dominant inferior frontal region and is associated with expressive language, while Wernicke area lies in the dominant posterior temporal region and is associated with receptive language. Parietal neglect and frontal executive/micturition/motor functions are consistent with the displayed clinical distinctions. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, The Anatomy of the Cerebral Cortex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK575742/), [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Cerebral Cortex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537247/), and [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Temporal Lobe](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519512/).

The visual-pathway reference supports questions 56 and 59. Optic-chiasm compression, including by a pituitary adenoma, produces bitemporal hemianopia. Temporal optic-radiation involvement affects Meyer’s loop and causes contralateral homonymous superior quadrantanopia. Sources: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Bitemporal Hemianopsia](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545213/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Visual Field Deficits](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10912/).

The NCBI posterior-circulation anatomy reference supports question 57: the basilar artery terminates by bifurcating into the posterior cerebral arteries. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Posterior Cerebral Arteries](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532260/).

The NCBI orbit/optic-nerve reference supports questions 58’s displayed answer: the ophthalmic artery supplies the optic nerve in the orbit and its extracranial/anterior portions. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Anatomy, Head and Neck, Orbit](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545161/).

The NCBI cerebellum reference supports question 60: dysmetria, ataxia, hypotonia, dysarthria/slurred speech, and intention tremor are cerebellar signs, whereas resting tremor is classically associated with parkinsonism. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Cerebellum](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537141/).
