## Evidence recorded

The NCBI Bookshelf visual-pathway reference, **Neuroanatomy, Visual Pathway**, states that each optic tract conveys visual information from the contralateral visual field of both eyes. It also describes predictable homonymous field defects after lesions along the optic radiations and the posterior visual pathway. This supports the displayed keys for page 1876 question 32, page 1878 question 34, and the explanations that distinguish optic-tract lesions from optic-chiasm lesions. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Visual Pathway](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK553189/).

The NCBI Bookshelf **Central Cord Syndrome** reference states that the syndrome commonly occurs in older adults after low-energy cervical hyperextension injury and produces motor deficits disproportionately greater in the upper extremities than in the lower extremities, often with bladder dysfunction. This supports page 1881 question 37 and its displayed key, Central cord syndrome. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Central Cord Syndrome](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441932/).

## Additional authoritative corroboration

The NCBI chapter **The Anatomy of the Cerebral Cortex** states that the median longitudinal fissure separates the cerebral hemispheres, that the lateral ventricles communicate with the third ventricle through the anterior interventricular foramina, and that the frontal lobe lies anterior to the central sulcus and above the lateral sulcus. It also describes the central sulcus and lateral sulcus as major cortical landmarks. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, The Anatomy of the Cerebral Cortex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK575742/).

The NCBI **Neuroanatomy, Cerebral Hemisphere** reference states that the deep longitudinal fissure separates the cerebral hemispheres, the primary somatosensory cortex is in the postcentral gyrus of the parietal lobe, the primary auditory cortex is in the superior temporal gyrus, and the frontal lobe is anterior to the central sulcus and superior to the lateral fissure. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Cerebral Hemisphere](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549789/).

The NCBI **Neuroanatomy, Spinal Cord** reference identifies the lateral spinothalamic tract among the lateral funiculus tracts. The NCBI **Neuroanatomy, Spinothalamic Tract** reference identifies the lateral component as the pathway carrying pain and temperature; the NCBI search record for this source also states that pain and temperature pathways cross the midline and ascend on the opposite side. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Spinal Cord](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559056/) and [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Spinothalamic Tract](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507824/).

The NCBI **Neuroanatomy, Ventricular System** reference states that the lateral ventricles communicate with the third ventricle through the interventricular foramen of Monro. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Ventricular System](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532932/).

The NCBI **Neuroanatomy, Thalamus** reference states that the thalamus forms the upper and lateral walls of the third ventricle and that the medial geniculate nucleus projects to the primary auditory cortex in the temporal lobe. Source: [NCBI Bookshelf, Neuroanatomy, Thalamus](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542184/).

The peer-reviewed PMC review **Imaging of the Primary Visual Pathway based on Visual Deficits** provides a localization table: homonymous hemianopia corresponds to a contralateral optic-tract lesion, upper homonymous quadrantanopia to contralateral temporal optic radiations, lower homonymous quadrantanopia to contralateral parietal optic radiations, and homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing to the contralateral occipital lobe. Source: [Patel et al., Journal of Clinical Imaging Science, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8053434/).
